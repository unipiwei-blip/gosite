How hreflang Tells Google Which Language Version to Show

A bilingual website is more than two sets of pages. Search engines need to know which version belongs to which audience.

Published · 2026-11-27
Follow GOSITE Global for weekly notes on launching, indexing and maintaining business websites.

Give each language a fixed address

The Chinese and English versions should each have their own permanent URL. A switcher that rewrites one page in place leaves search engines with a single address and no clear language signal.

Declare the relationship

Language annotations in the page head and sitemap tell Google that two URLs are versions of the same page, not duplicates. Each version points back to the other, with a default version for visitors who match neither.

Rewrite, do not translate

Keyword research should be done separately for each market. Words buyers use in English differ from a literal translation of Chinese copy. Titles, descriptions and body text should be written for the reader in that language.

Keep signals consistent

Language attributes, navigation, contact details, currency and time zone should all point to the same market. Mixed signals make a page look like a translated copy rather than a real local presence.

Static sites make this simple

Static pages have fixed addresses and no changing routes, so language annotations stay stable. The whole structure can be delivered at launch rather than added later.
